# Contraception options

An overview of the methods available in India. A doctor can help you choose the right one.

Every method has trade-offs. Talk to a doctor about what suits your health, your plans and your lifestyle. Many methods are available **free at government health centres**.

## Short-acting methods

- **Condoms:** the only method that also protects against sexually transmitted infections. Use one every time.
- **Combined oral pills:** a pill taken daily. Very effective when taken correctly.
- **Chhaya (centchroman):** a non-hormonal pill taken weekly after the first 3 months. Available free in the government programme.
- **Antara (injectable):** a hormonal injection given every 3 months.

## Long-acting methods

- **Copper IUD (Copper-T):** a small device placed in the uterus by a doctor. Lasts 5–10 years depending on the type, and can be removed whenever you want.
- **Hormonal IUD:** lasts several years and often makes periods lighter.

## Permanent methods

- **Female sterilisation (tubectomy)** and **male sterilisation (vasectomy)**. Vasectomy is a simpler and quicker procedure.

## Emergency contraception

If you had unprotected sex or a condom broke, an emergency pill can help prevent pregnancy. Take it **as soon as possible, within 72 hours**. It is sold at pharmacies without a prescription. It is not meant for regular use.

## Why not cycle tracking?

Calendar-based methods are much less reliable than the options above. **Do not use Sakhi's fertile window as contraception.**
