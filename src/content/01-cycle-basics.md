# Your cycle, phase by phase

What happens in your body across one menstrual cycle, and what counts as normal.

A cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. For adults, anything from **21 to 35 days** is considered normal, and it is common for your cycle to vary by a few days each month.

## The four phases

- **Menstruation (about days 1–5):** the lining of the uterus sheds. Bleeding usually lasts 2–7 days. Cramps and low energy are common.
- **Follicular phase (period until ovulation):** oestrogen rises and an egg matures. Many people feel more energetic in this phase.
- **Ovulation (around 14 days before your next period):** an egg is released. You may notice clear, stretchy discharge, like raw egg white, and sometimes mild one-sided pain.
- **Luteal phase (after ovulation):** progesterone rises. If there is no pregnancy, hormone levels fall and your period starts. Bloating, breast tenderness and mood changes (PMS) are common in the last few days.

## Why predictions are estimates

Ovulation does not always happen on the same day. Stress, illness, travel, weight change, breastfeeding and some medicines can shift your cycle. That is why Sakhi shows a likely range, and why the fertile window **must not be used as contraception**.

## When to see a doctor

- Cycles regularly shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days
- No period for 3 months (and you are not pregnant)
- Bleeding that lasts longer than 7 days, or soaks a pad every 1–2 hours
- Bleeding between periods or after sex
- Pain that stops you from going to work, school or doing daily tasks
