# Endometriosis: when period pain is not "normal"

Severe period pain is common, but it is not something you simply have to put up with.

Endometriosis is when tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside it, for example on the ovaries or the bowel. It causes inflammation and pain. On average it takes years to be diagnosed, often because severe pain is dismissed as normal.

## Signs to watch for

- Period pain that painkillers do not control, or that makes you miss school or work
- Pain that starts before your period and lasts after it
- Pain during or after sex
- Pain when passing stool or urine, especially during your period
- Heavy periods, or bleeding between periods
- Difficulty getting pregnant
- Tiredness that does not go away

## Diagnosis and treatment

A doctor may suggest an ultrasound or MRI. A laparoscopy (keyhole surgery) can confirm it. Treatment includes pain relief, hormonal medicines that reduce or stop periods, and sometimes surgery. There is no cure yet, but symptoms can be controlled well.

## When to see a doctor

If period pain regularly affects your daily life, see a gynaecologist. Keeping a record of your periods and pain in Sakhi helps you explain the pattern at your appointment.
