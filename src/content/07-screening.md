# Health checks every woman should know

Simple screenings that catch cervical and breast cancer early, when they are most treatable.

## Cervical cancer

Cervical cancer is one of the most common cancers in women in India, and one of the most preventable.

- **HPV vaccine:** protects against the virus that causes most cervical cancers. It works best at ages 9–14, before any sexual contact. Ask your doctor about catch-up doses if you are older.
- **Screening:** from age 30, ask your doctor about screening. This can be an HPV test, a Pap smear or a VIA test, which is available free at many government health centres. How often you need it depends on the test, usually every 3–5 years.
- **See a doctor if** you have bleeding between periods, after sex or after menopause, or unusual discharge with a bad smell.

## Breast cancer

Breast cancer is the most common cancer among women in India, and it is often found late.

- **Know your normal:** look at and feel your breasts regularly, for example once a month a few days after your period ends.
- **Check for:** a lump or thickening in the breast or armpit, a change in size or shape, skin dimpling or puckering, a nipple turning inwards, or any discharge from the nipple.
- **Clinical breast exam:** ask your doctor to examine your breasts during regular check-ups. They may suggest a mammogram, usually from age 40 or earlier if breast cancer runs in your family.

Most breast lumps are not cancer, but always get a new lump checked.

## Other checks

- Blood pressure, blood sugar and haemoglobin: at least once a year
- Thyroid: if you have irregular periods, tiredness or weight changes
